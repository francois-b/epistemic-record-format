The cost per unit of computation has continued to fall exponentially, which is the generalization of Moore's law the essay relies on.

## Working notes

The one premise of the essay that is straightforwardly measurable, and the measurement mostly holds: Epoch AI's 470-GPU dataset gives FLOP/s per dollar doubling about every 2.5 years across 2006 to 2021, which is exponential, though slightly slower than Moore's two-year figure.

The against side is about the derivative, not the direction. Transistor scaling slowed after about 2010, the industry's own roadmap body stopped organising around the law in 2016, and Moore himself put the end around 2025. Brooks, writing six days after the essay, gives a doubling time of twenty years rather than one. Nothing here shows the fall has stopped; it shows the exponent is contested and the essay does not say which exponent it needs.
