Building in human knowledge and leveraging computation compete in practice, because time spent on one is time not spent on the other and knowledge-heavy methods are harder to scale.

## Working notes

The essay concedes the two need not conflict and then asserts that in practice they do. Every atom against is the same objection from a different direction: the systems that won did both at once. AlexNet's authors say in as many words that their model needs lots of prior knowledge; Brooks says the convolution front end, the labelled dataset and the architecture choice are all human knowledge that has been moved rather than removed.
