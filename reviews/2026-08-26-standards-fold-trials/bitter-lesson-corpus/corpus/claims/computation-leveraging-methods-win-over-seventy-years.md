Over seventy years of AI research, general methods that leverage computation have ultimately proved the most effective, and by a large margin.

## Working notes

This is the essay's conclusion and everything else in the corpus is either a premise of it or an attack on one. It is an argument rather than an observation because no measurement settles it: the four cases are the evidence, the word 'ultimately' is the hinge, and Nielsen's objection is that the hinge fixes no horizon and so decides nothing about what to do now.

The strongest attack on record is not that the cases are wrong but that they are compatible with a different explanation. Hooker's hardware lottery reads the same seventy years as ideas winning because they suited the available silicon; Brooks reads them as every solution having cost substantial human ingenuity somewhere, with the ingenuity relocated rather than removed. Neither is modelled here as a rival claim, per ERF-23; both sit in atoms_against.
