Buffon's Histoire Naturelle asserts that New World quadrupeds are, as
a class, diminished or degenerate relative to their Old World
counterparts.

## Working notes

The two supporting atoms are specific, textual instances of the general
claim: bjd-001 shows Buffon classing a New World species (the pecari)
as itself a *descended, diminished* form of an Old World animal (the
hog) rather than as a native New World type on its own footing; bjd-002
shows Buffon's own size comparison for the tapir, the largest quadruped
he grants to the New World, against the elephant that holds the
equivalent rank in the Old World.

bjd-003 is recorded against this claim on purpose. It is a passage from
the *same* essay, on the *same* general subject, where Buffon's own text
runs the other way: more polecat kinds in America than in Europe, and
the American nature judged the better one. Buffon's degeneracy thesis,
read in the primary text rather than in its later flattened retellings,
is not applied uniformly -- this is worth keeping visible rather than
smoothing over, and is one of the reasons this claim is typed
`observation` (checkable against the atoms as they actually read) rather
than paraphrased as some single crisp Buffon "law".

This claim's scope is narrower than "Buffon believed America was bad":
it is specifically about the *size/diminution* strand of the thesis
(the strand Jefferson's weight tables answer directly), not about
Buffon's separate and much harsher claims about Native Americans (Query
VI's own text engages that strand too, in a passage not captured for
this corpus).
