No document contemporaneous with 1787-88 -- a Buffon-authored passage,
a letter, a press notice -- confirms that Buffon retracted or revised
the degeneracy thesis after receiving Jefferson's moose; the popular
"he promised, then died" story rests on a single Jefferson
recollection reported roughly 37 years later, in 1824.

## Working notes

This is a universal-negative-shaped claim (ERF-25): no atom can prove
an absence, only survey coverage can. It cites
`bjd-buffon-retraction-survey-2026-08-25` rather than atoms, following
the spec's own recommended pattern for this shape of claim.

The survey's own coverage-bounds note applies directly here: four
general web searches are not a search of the Histoire Naturelle's own
posthumous volumes or of 1787-88 Parisian periodicals, so this claim's
"no document confirms" is read as *searched-and-not-found within a
general web index*, not as *established true of the entire surviving
record*. That scoping is the entire point of typing this claim
`observation` with `surveys` attached rather than overclaiming a proven
negative.

Two things the survey did locate matter for how this claim should be
read, and are recorded in the survey's own `notable_results` rather
than restated as atoms here (no atom was minted for either, since
neither is quoted from a captured primary source in this corpus): (1)
a widely repeated but so far primary-unattested quotation ("I should
have consulted you, Sir..."), and (2) the actual origin of the "next
volume" promise, which traces to Jefferson's 1824 account to Daniel
Webster -- a real, locatable trace, but a decades-later, third-hand
one, not a document from the moose's actual arrival.
