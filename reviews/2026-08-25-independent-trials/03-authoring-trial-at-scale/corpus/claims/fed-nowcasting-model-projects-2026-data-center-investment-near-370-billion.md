## Working notes

Federal Reserve Board economists' project-level nowcasting model, validated against national-accounts history, forecasts aggregate US data-center investment reaching $370 billion annualized by mid-2026, with a 2027 forecast ranging from $360 billion to $930 billion depending on the assumed pace of future project announcements.

The Fed-paper investment-magnitude claim the task brief asked for, distinct from macro-productivity-effects-of-ai-are-modest-per-current-estimates, which cites a different FRB source (n-frb-notes-ai-buildout-2026) on productivity diffusion rather than investment-flow forecasting. Federal Reserve Board economists' project-level nowcasting model forecasts aggregate U.S. data-center investment reaching $370 billion annualized by 2026:Q2, with a 2027 forecast ranging from $360 billion to $930 billion depending on the assumed pace of future project announcements (acx-111); the paper's method is built from abandonment rates, time from plan-to-start, and time from start-to-completion, and the authors validate it by showing it reproduces the recent history of aggregate data-center investment recorded in the national accounts (acx-121) -- included specifically because the methodology note is what makes the $370B figure worth treating as a serious independent estimate rather than another guess. atoms_against carries Futurum's $660-690B aggregate 2026 hyperscaler-capex estimate (acx-44, already used and flagged for its own divergence from J.P. Morgan's figure in capex-hyperscaler-aggregate-2026-doubling): the Fed's $370B figure and the hyperscaler-aggregate figures are not measuring the same universe (national-accounts "data center investment" spans more than five hyperscalers' total capex, much of which is not datacenter-specific), so the roughly 2x gap between them is a genuine measurement-definition tension worth surfacing, in the same spirit as the GMO/Futurum tension the prior author already flagged, rather than a straightforward contradiction to resolve.
