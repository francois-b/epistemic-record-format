## Working notes

Large prepaid and customer-supplied-hardware components of AI infrastructure contracts, and direct multi-year compute purchase commitments from well-capitalized customers, reduce the vendor's own capital-raising burden and are cited by the companies involved as evidence the spending is demand-backed.

Oracle states its $75B of prepaid and customer-supplied-hardware contract terms 'substantially reduces' its own capital-raising need (acx-7); Jassy explicitly frames Amazon's ~$200B 2026 capex as not 'on a hunch,' citing OpenAI's over-$100B commitment as demand backing (acx-9); Anthropic committed over $100B over ten years to AWS for compute capacity (acx-40) and has 100,000+ customers already running Claude on Bedrock via a live joint compute cluster with over one million Trainium2 chips (acx-142), not just a forward commitment; Anthropic separately committed $30B to Azure compute (acx-15). Framed carefully as 'cited by the companies involved as evidence,' because that is what the atoms show (self-interested parties describing their own contracts favorably), not an independent finding that the spending is in fact demand-backed. atoms_against carries the circular-financing counter-read: NVIDIA guaranteeing payment for CoreWeave's unsold GPU capacity (acx-51) and NVIDIA's own guarantee for the OpenAI Ohio campus being negotiated down from a reported ~$250B consideration to $105B (acx-52) both complicate a clean 'buyer conviction' reading, since in these cases the entity providing the 'demand signal' is partly the same entity supplying the capacity.
