**Abstract.** We examine the early labor market impacts of AI chatbots using two large-scale adoption surveys of 11 exposed occupations, linked to Danish administrative data. Chatbots are now widespread: most employers encourage use, many deploy enterprise solutions, and training is common. These initiatives boost adoption, narrow gender gaps, and create new tasks. Yet, impacts on earnings and recorded hours remain minimal. Using difference-in-differences, we estimate precise null effects at both the individual and workplace levels, with confidence intervals ruling out effects above 1%. While adoption is associated with occupational switching and task restructuring, these shifts do not result in net changes to hours or earnings. Modest productivity gains (3% time savings) and the emergence of integration and oversight tasks help explain these muted effects. Our findings challenge narratives of imminent disruption from Generative AI, highlighting instead the slow burn of productivity J-curves.

The emergence of AI chatbots marks the rise of Generative Artificial Intelligence (AI). By some measures, these technologies are already living up to the immense hype: AI chatbots have seen the fastest worker take-up of any new technology [...], randomized controlled trials (RCTs) demonstrate substantial productivity gains for users [...], and case studies indicate notable effects on online labor market platforms [...]. These effects on productivity and platform demand are remarkable in both magnitude—ranging from 15% to 50%—and speed, materializing within a few months.
