# B-61 · A narrative cannot name the source it is drawn from

In a corpus whose subject is one document, that document is held twice:
as the narrative (schema `Narrative`, bound to claims under `ERF-31`) and
as a source's normalized text (`ERF-1`, quoted by atoms). No field relates
the two. `Narrative` carries `title`, `corpus`, `created` and a body, and
nothing that says "this prose is, or is drawn from, source S".

Two consequences follow, and both trials met one each.

**The narrative can drift from its source.** The quote check runs against
the normalized text; the anchor check runs against the narrative;
`ERF-32` detects a claim moving under a binding. Nothing detects the
narrative's prose diverging from the text it was minted from. The essay
trial generated the narrative from the normalized text by tool, which is
a discipline the format does not ask for.

**The proponent's own assertion reads as backing.** Each bound claim wants
an atom recording that the essay asserts it, otherwise the binding points
at a claim with no trace of its origin. `ERF-10` grades a recorded
identified utterance `high`, correctly. At the claim level the arithmetic
then misleads: one `high` atom for, nine against, where the one for is
high-grade evidence that the author said it and no evidence that it is
true. Nothing marks an atom as the proponent's own statement, and nothing
can flag a claim whose only atoms for quote the source the claim was read
out of, because the model does not know which source that is.

The second cannot be computed until the first is stated, which is why the
two findings are one entry.

## Proposed resolution

`Narrative` gains an optional `source` (a source id, resolving under
`ERF-35` as a current reference). With it a consumer MAY flag divergence
between the narrative body and the source's normalized text under the
fold, and MAY flag a claim whose every atom for names that source. The
alternative, craft guidance only (a discourse atom's finding says "X
asserts", and readers read findings rather than count grades), is what
holds today and is what the second trial found insufficient.
