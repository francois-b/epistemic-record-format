W3C PROV models a thing's history as entities, activities, and agents, and
defines attribution as ascribing an entity to an agent.

The standard is a decade old, carries W3C Recommendation status, and its
four core definitions are quoted here from the Recommendations themselves.
Entities are deliberately broad, spanning physical, digital, and conceptual
things and admitting imaginary ones. Activities occur over time and act upon
entities. Agents bear responsibility. Attribution ties an entity to the
agent answerable for it.

## Working notes

This claim exists in the example corpus for a reason beyond its content: its
four atoms are the only ones here whose captured copies could be
redistributed, so it is the one claim on which the mechanical quote check
actually runs for a reader of the published repository. Everything else in
this corpus is honest about being uncheckable from where you are standing.

What the claim deliberately does not say is anything about what PROV omits.
A reader may notice that these definitions describe who did what to a thing
rather than whether a statement follows from its source, and that
observation is what the surrounding narrative draws out. But an absence is
not established by quoting what a document does say, and this format is
strict about that: a claim about what a body of work lacks wants a survey,
not a pile of atoms.
