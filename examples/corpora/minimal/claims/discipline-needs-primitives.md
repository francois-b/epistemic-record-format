The claim: coding's shared quality culture (code review, CI, lint, evals)
institutionalized because composable mechanical primitives made each
discipline cheap to practice and hard to fake — diff makes a change
reviewable as a delta, the test runner makes correctness machine-checkable,
the parser makes style mechanically enforceable. Each discipline is a social
practice wrapped around a primitive. Knowledge work never grew the
equivalent disciplines because the equivalent primitives don't exist: no
diff-for-arguments, no test-runner-for-claims, no lint-for-provenance.
Consequences the claim explains: (a) the funded "AI for knowledge work"
companies (Harvey, Glean, Hebbia) all built retrieval/summarization
products — corpora monetize, primitives don't; (b) every historical
knowledge-work discipline (citators, fact-checking desks, PSLs) had to be
STAFFED — humans were the primitives, which is why those institutions were
expensive and rare (edge: km-institutions-consequence-club); (c) building
the discipline for one's own practice requires building primitives first —
atoms/claims/juries/registries are exactly that.

Provenance of the synthesis: produced 2026-07-22 by two adversarial rounds
on an earlier draft thesis — an LLM's narrowing of it ("tools exist,
discipline doesn't") composed with the author's opposing read ("the tools
are the wrong category; there are no primitives"). The two
findings are causally linked, not parallel.
