Abstract. Data center investment in the U.S. has increased rapidly in the post-pandemic era,
and plans for future investment have surged further. Forecasting investment at such a turning
point is an important but potentially fraught exercise, especially given lags in aggregate data
availability. We develop a straightforward method to forecast aggregate investment using
project-level microdata and a small number of parameters: specifically, abandonment rates, time
from plan-to-start, and time from start-to-completion. As a key validation of our approach, we
generate estimates that match the recent history of aggregate data center investment in the
NIPAs. We then use our method to generate nowcasts of aggregate data center investment in the
short run, with the mean forecast indicating that investment will increase to $370 billion
annualized by 2026:Q2. We can extend our methodology further out, but our forecasts then become
conditional on the assumed flow of new data center plans. Assuming future plans range from
one-fourth to twice the average pace of plans from 2024-2025 implies a range of investment
forecasts of $360 billion to $930 billion in 2027, demonstrating the substantial upside and
downside risks to future levels of investment. Keywords: data centers, commercial real estate,
forecasting, construction, time-to-plan.
