## Working notes

As of 2025, the large majority of enterprise generative-AI pilots and deployments are producing no measurable financial return, despite tens of billions of dollars in enterprise investment.

This is MIT Project NANDA's headline finding: despite $30-40B in enterprise generative-AI investment, 95% of organizations report zero measurable return (acx-24, the 'GenAI Divide'), with the drop-off concentrated at the enterprise-grade custom/vendor-system stage -- 80%+ have piloted consumer tools like ChatGPT or Copilot, but of the organizations evaluating enterprise-grade systems specifically, only 20% reach pilot and just 5% reach production (acx-25); acx-85 gives the study's own diagnosis (see genai-divide-attributed-to-learning-not-infrastructure-limits). atoms_against carries Humlum and Vestergaard's Danish administrative-data finding of modest (~3%) individual time-savings from chatbot adoption (acx-58) as a genuine complication rather than a clean rebuttal: it measures worker-level time savings in a research design built to detect earnings/hours effects, not enterprise-level financial ROI on formal AI deployments, so the two studies are not measuring quite the same thing -- flagged in claimsA-friction.md.
