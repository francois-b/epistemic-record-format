## Working notes

By Sequoia's David Cahn's revenue-gap methodology, applied to hyperscaler and AI infrastructure spending, the AI industry needs several hundred billion to several trillion dollars in additional annual AI-specific revenue beyond what is currently realized to justify the pace of the buildout.

Tracked across Cahn's own escalating figures: a 2023 '$125B hole' (acx-86), a mid-2024 '$600B question' built by taking NVIDIA's run-rate revenue forecast and multiplying roughly 4x for total data-center cost and resale margin (acx-18), a parallel '$500B hole' using his own generous per-company AI-revenue assumptions (acx-19), the 'TSMC Brake' observation that TSMC's capex grew only 10% while its revenue grew 50% since 2022, a structural constraint on how fast capacity can scale (acx-20), a 2025 '$8 trillion question' at $40B/GW for a hypothetical 100 GW buildout (acx-45), and his entering-2026 statement that realized AI end revenue remains 'tens of billions' per year against 'trillions' of five-year infrastructure investment (acx-87). Graded as observation rather than argument because the claim is about what Cahn's stated methodology and its outputs are and whether the atoms support that characterization, which is checkable directly against the quoted essays; it does not itself adjudicate whether Cahn's 4x multiplier or generosity assumptions are the right ones to use. atoms_against carries the RPO figures (Microsoft, Oracle) that Cahn's revenue-need framing, built around near-term realized revenue, does not count as revenue yet -- critics of Cahn's methodology would point to exactly these contracted-but-unrealized figures as reason his 'hole' is overstated.
