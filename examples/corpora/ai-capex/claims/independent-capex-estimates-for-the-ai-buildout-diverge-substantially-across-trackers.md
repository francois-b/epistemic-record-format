## Working notes

Independent trackers of AI-related data-center capital expenditure, including Bain, Synergy Research, and Dell'Oro, report substantially different figures and growth rates for the same buildout, indicating no single settled 'the capex number' exists even among specialist industry analysts.

capex-hyperscaler-aggregate-2026-doubling already flags that Futurum's ($660-690B) and J.P. Morgan's ($697B) aggregates do not fully agree with each other or with GMO/Chancellor's ($300B for four companies, 1.3-1.6% of GDP); this claim widens the same point with three further, previously unused trackers whose figures do not slot neatly onto that spread either. Bain estimates the data-center buildout needed to meet anticipated AI demand requires about $500 billion in annual capital investment, which its own capex-to-revenue analysis implies requires $2 trillion in annual revenue to sustain, and calculates that even shifting all on-premise IT budgets to cloud plus reinvesting projected AI productivity savings would leave that revenue need $800 billion short (acx-55) -- a demand-side, revenue-need-implying estimate, not a simple capex tally. Synergy Research reports quarterly hyperscale-operator capex grew almost 180% over three years to $142 billion in Q3 2025 alone (acx-56); Dell'Oro reports worldwide data-center capex grew 57% in 2025 (acx-57). None of these three exactly reconciles with the $660-700B 2026 aggregate, the $370B Fed nowcast, or each other, in scope (worldwide vs hyperscaler-only vs need-implied), time period, or methodology. atoms_against carries the two aggregate estimates already on record (acx-44, acx-49) as the comparison points this divergence is measured against, not as evidence contradicting any single figure -- the point of this claim is the spread itself, which cuts against confident claims from either camp that "the" capex number is settled and therefore that its implications (bubble or sustainable) are settled either.
