## Working notes

ERCOT's Large Load Integration Team's 137 new interconnection submissions, totaling approximately 140,000 MW of new large load requested by 2036, is roughly fifteen times the peak consumption ERCOT currently observes from large loads already approved to energize.

This is the explicit interconnection-queue claim the task brief asked for, and it was previously absent: reported-contracted-capacity-figures-overstate-realized-electricity-demand cites Dominion's contracted-vs-forecast gap and ERCOT's approved-vs-observed gap, but not ERCOT's forward interconnection queue, which is a distinct and much larger figure than either. ERCOT's Large Load Integration Team reports having recently received 137 new Large Load Interconnection submissions, which its preliminary review indicates total approximately 140,000 MW of new large load by 2036 (acx-120). Set against ERCOT's own observed non-simultaneous monthly peak consumption of 3,883 MW against 9,042 MW of loads that have already received Approval to Energize (acx-101, dated March 2025 in the source), the queue figure is roughly 15x the already-approved capacity and roughly 36x currently observed peak draw -- a scale gap one order of magnitude larger than the approved-vs-observed gap that atom already documents on its own. This claim states the fact of the gap without adjudicating what it means; the further inferential step -- whether a queue this size should be read as a genuine demand signal or as mostly speculative -- is taken up by interconnection-queue-size-likely-overstates-binding-demand-like-contracted-capacity-does, which assumes this claim as a premise. No atoms_against: this is a straightforward comparison of two disclosed figures from the same grid operator, not itself a contested reading.
