## Working notes

AI's cost structure is expensive enough, relative to the kinds of problems current AI systems reliably solve, that whether the technology will justify its buildout cost the way earlier low-cost technology transitions such as the early internet did remains genuinely unresolved.

Restates the substance of Jim Covello's Goldman Sachs argument (a >$1T buildout that must solve complex, high-value problems to be worth its cost, which he argues it is not designed to do, acx-16, acx-17; his framing that replacing low-wage jobs with tremendously costly AI is the 'polar opposite' of prior tech transitions he has witnessed, acx-80) as a standalone, disagreeable proposition rather than as a report of what Covello said. Graded as a bet, not an observation or argument: whether AI 'justifies its cost' is a forward-looking question about what the technology will actually deliver, which only the world -- realized value delivered over the coming years -- will settle; no atom in this corpus, on either side, closes that question now. atoms_against gives the strongest counter-signals available to date: adoption speed unlike any prior technology (ChatGPT to 900M+ weekly users, acx-151) and a macro data-point that AI/data-center investment already accounted for 80% of the increase in US final private domestic demand in H1 2025 (acx-105) -- both cut against 'not designed to solve valuable problems,' though neither settles whether the value delivered clears Covello's cost bar.
