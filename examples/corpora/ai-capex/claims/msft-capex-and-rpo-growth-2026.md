## Working notes

Microsoft's fiscal 2026 capital expenditure rose roughly 80% year-over-year to $115.9 billion in property-and-equipment additions, alongside commercial remaining performance obligation growth of 84% to $678 billion, of which non-OpenAI RPO growth was 25%.

acx-1 (10-K capex figure), acx-3 (RPO growth, ex-OpenAI split), acx-4 (useful-life extension and the resulting CY2026 capex guidance of ~$175B), acx-61 (Q4 capex of $41B, roughly two-thirds short-lived assets, i.e. CPUs/GPUs -- included because it shows the composition of spend, not just the total). One of two per-company claims decomposed out of capex-hyperscaler-aggregate-2026-doubling. atoms_against carries Microsoft's own 10-K risk-factor language: that AI/cloud capital investments are made ahead of fully developed revenue streams and revenue may not be realized in the expected timeframe or level (acx-2), and that customers may reduce, delay, or shift AI workloads away from Azure, with slower-than-expected adoption meaning Microsoft may not realize expected returns (acx-60) -- both are Microsoft's own stated risk factors, not an outside critic's view, and are included precisely because a company's own risk-factor language is unusually direct evidence against a rosy reading of its own numbers.
