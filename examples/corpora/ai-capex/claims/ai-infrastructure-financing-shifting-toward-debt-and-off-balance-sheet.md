## Working notes

A growing share of AI infrastructure spending is financed through debt issuance, off-balance-sheet leases, and third-party guarantees rather than internally generated cash, and credit markets are beginning to show signs of strain absorbing it.

Evidence spans a single company's balance sheet (Oracle: $260B of off-balance-sheet lease commitments, acx-6; $43B debt plus $5B equity raised in FY2026, acx-63) through to system-wide bond issuance (hyperscalers and related entities issued $225B in bonds by mid-2026, a 973.7% jump, on pace for $400B for the year, acx-116; 'hidden' off-balance-sheet debt estimated at $1.2-1.65T by Nikkei/Moody's, acx-117) and an explicit credit-market-fatigue signal from S&P (acx-138). The NVIDIA guarantee for OpenAI's Ohio campus, finalized at up to $105B after reportedly being considered at up to $250B (acx-52), is included as financing-structure evidence, not demand evidence -- a guarantee is a credit instrument. atoms_against carries Oracle's own framing that its $75B of prepaid and customer-supplied-hardware contract terms 'substantially reduces' the capital Oracle itself must raise (acx-7): the same company is both raising debt (acx-63) and shrinking its own funding need through prepayment (acx-7), which is a real tension in Oracle's own disclosures, not a contradiction to paper over.
