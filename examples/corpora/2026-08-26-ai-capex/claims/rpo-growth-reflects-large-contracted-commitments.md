## Working notes

Remaining performance obligations and cloud backlog at major AI-infrastructure vendors grew sharply in 2026, reflecting large multi-year customer commitments recorded on the vendors' own books.

Microsoft's commercial RPO grew 84% YoY to $678B (acx-3); Oracle's RPO grew from $138B to $638B, up 363% (acx-5, acx-148); CoreWeave's RPO reached $103.7B with 41% expected to convert to revenue within 24 months (acx-37); Google Cloud backlog grew to $514B (acx-70). These are disclosed, audited figures, not analyst estimates. atoms_against carries Gary Marcus's specific challenge to the Oracle figure (acx-46: the deal driving much of Oracle's RPO growth is 'apparently non-binding' with a customer Marcus says cannot pay) -- a challenge to the *quality* of one company's RPO, not to whether RPO grew. The claim that RPO growth is real and the claim that RPO growth doesn't establish monetizable, diversified demand (rpo-growth-does-not-establish-monetizable-diversified-demand) are both true at once; that pairing is exactly the point, which is why they carry a conflicts-with edge rather than one superseding the other.
