## Working notes

By Aswath Damodaran's breakeven arithmetic, an aggregated $5 trillion market pricing for AI product and service companies at a blended 20% operating margin requires $5 trillion in annual revenue to break even over a 10-year horizon, rising to more than $8 trillion over 15 years, figures well beyond any currently realized AI revenue on record in this corpus.

A third, independent revenue-gap methodology alongside Cahn's and Zitron's, and one not yet in the corpus: where Cahn works from NVIDIA's run-rate revenue multiplied for total data-center cost and resale margin, and Zitron tallies five companies' capex against their narrowly defined AI revenue, Damodaran works backward from aggregate market pricing of AI-labeled companies to the revenue a 20% operating margin would need to justify that pricing at a given discount horizon. Aswath Damodaran calculates that, at an aggregated $5 trillion market pricing for AI product and service companies and a blended 20% operating margin, the industry would need $5 trillion in annual revenue to break even on a 10-year horizon, rising to more than $8 trillion on a 15-year horizon (acx-48). Graded as observation, since the claim is about what Damodaran's stated arithmetic and its inputs are, checkable directly, not an independent judgment on whether $5 trillion is the right market-pricing input or 20% the right margin assumption. Carries a supports edge into ai-capex-outpaces-realizable-revenue-bubble-risk, giving that argument a further, independently-derived premise route distinct from its existing Cahn and Zitron premises. atoms_against carries Google Cloud's $514 billion backlog (acx-70, reused from cahn-revenue-gap-methodology-and-figures's own atoms_against, where it plays the same role): large contracted-but-unrealized figures are exactly what a critic of any of these three revenue-need methodologies -- Damodaran's included -- would point to as reason the "hole" looks larger than it is, since none of these approaches count backlog as revenue yet.
