## Working notes

Existing macroeconomic modeling of AI's aggregate effects implies a middle reading that cuts against both camps' strongest rhetoric: the projected productivity gains are too modest to support claims of imminent transformative growth, yet still represent a real, positive economic effect at the buildout's capital scale, which is inconsistent with a reading that the investment is purely wasteful.

Assumes macro-productivity-effects-of-ai-are-modest-per-current-estimates as its premise (Acemoglu's 10-year TFP ceiling of 0.66%, revised down toward 0.53%) and reasons past it explicitly rather than simply restating it: "modest" cuts two ways at once, against the strongest transformative-growth framing some AI proponents use, and against a reading that current investment is delivering nothing of macroeconomic substance. Illustrative atoms_for: Jones's weak-link scenario, in which per-capita growth could exceed 5%/year if AI eventually automates away nearly all weak links (acx-133, already atoms_against on the premise claim itself, reused here as evidence the "not purely wasteful" half of this argument is not merely asserted but has a serious long-run economic case behind it), and S&P Global's finding that data-center and AI-related investment accounted for 80% of the increase in US final private domestic demand in H1 2025 (acx-105) -- a concrete, already-realized macro footprint, not a projection, that a "purely wasteful" reading would have to explain away. atoms_against carries the sharpest complication: MIT NANDA's finding that 95% of organizations report zero measurable return despite $30-40B in enterprise investment (acx-24) is real evidence pulling toward the more pessimistic end of "purely wasteful" at the microeconomic level, even though this claim's macro framing is about aggregate productivity and investment footprint, not enterprise-level ROI specifically -- the two are not measuring quite the same thing, similar to the Humlum/NANDA tension the prior author already flagged elsewhere, and that gap is stated rather than papered over.
