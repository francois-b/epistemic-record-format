## Working notes

Ed Zitron's tally finds that Meta, Amazon, Microsoft, Google, and Tesla together spent over $560 billion on AI-related capital expenditure between 2024 and 2025 while generating only about $35 billion in AI revenue combined.

The headline figure is acx-21; supporting detail is Zitron's own $80B Microsoft 2025 capex line-item (acx-81), the OpenAI-discount finding narrowing Microsoft's clean AI revenue further (acx-22, low source_quality, flagged), and Anthropic's projected $2.7B 2024 loss as evidence for Zitron's broader argument that leading LLM providers price aggressively rather than for profit (acx-23, low source_quality). This claim reports Zitron's tally as a tally -- what his stated methodology counts as 'AI revenue' is narrow (frontier-model-attributable revenue), which is exactly why the atoms_against matters: Microsoft's full fiscal-year cloud revenue topped $214B with nearly 90% from customers outside frontier model companies (acx-62), and AWS operating income rose to $16.6B in a single quarter (acx-67) -- neither of which Zitron's narrower AI-revenue definition would count, but both of which are real revenue the capex is also buying capacity for. The tension is definitional (what counts as 'AI revenue') as much as factual, and is called out rather than resolved.
