## Working notes

Hogendorn's finding that the 1990s telecom boom's 'excessive entry' was concentrated in carrier swaps and leases rather than physical construction parallels the present-day concern that reported AI-era backlog and capacity figures can be inflated through financial and contractual structuring rather than only through genuine physical overbuild.

historical-technology-buildout-analogies-are-ambiguous-precedent is deliberately left without edges into the central sustainable-vs-bubble pair, and its own working notes explain why: the atoms genuinely support neither side more than the other at that claim's level of generality, and forcing an edge there would misrepresent the evidence. Rereading the atoms with the task brief's specific question in mind -- is there a real, narrower relation the neutral claim's own evidence licenses, even if the claim itself should stay neutral -- I think there is one, and this claim states it without touching the parent. Christiaan Hogendorn's study of the US long-distance fiber-optic buildout concludes that "excessive entry" during the telecom boom was concentrated in swaps and leases between carriers rather than in the physical construction of network capacity, which appears far more moderate once shared route-miles are excluded (acx-115). That specific mechanism -- apparent capacity or activity inflated by financial/contractual structuring between firms, not by what actually got built -- is structurally the same complaint rpo-growth-does-not-establish-monetizable-diversified-demand makes about today's RPO and backlog figures (the Oracle deal characterized as apparently non-binding, the circular NVIDIA-CoreWeave-OpenAI guarantee structures). Assumes historical-technology-buildout-analogies-are-ambiguous-precedent as its premise (an observation, a terminal leaf) and supports rpo-growth-does-not-establish-monetizable-diversified-demand directly: this is a narrow, mechanism-level connection, not a claim that the historical episode as a whole favors the bear case, which is why it does not touch the parent claim's own edges or its neutrality. atoms_against carries Odlyzko's own caution against reading the post-dot-com mood swing toward unjustified pessimism (acx-125, reused from the parent claim's atoms_for): if the lesson of that era's aftermath was that observers over-corrected into excess bearishness, that is a real caution against leaning too hard on this particular historical parallel as confirmation of present-day bear concerns.
