## Working notes

Free cash flow at Amazon, Oracle, and Alphabet fell sharply or turned negative in 2025-2026 as AI-related capital expenditure accelerated, while Microsoft's free cash flow grew despite comparable capex growth.

This is deliberately cross-company rather than single-firm: Amazon's TTM FCF went to an outflow of $7.6B (acx-11) and full-year FCF fell from $38B to $11B (acx-65); Oracle posted FY2026 FCF of negative $23.7B (acx-8); Alphabet reported negative $5.9B FCF for Q2 2026 alone (acx-31); Meta's cost growth (55%) is outrunning its revenue growth (28%) in the same quarter (acx-72). Microsoft is the outlier evidenced against this pattern: FY2026 Q4 free cash flow was $19.6B, up, even as capex rose (acx-147), and its cloud revenue and operating income kept climbing (acx-62, acx-67 -- the latter is AWS operating income, included here because it's the clearest 'capex funding a genuinely profitable, growing segment' counter-data-point across the set). The honest reading is not 'capex is unsustainable' or 'capex is fine' but that the FCF picture diverges sharply by company, which is exactly the kind of texture a single aggregate capex number destroys.
