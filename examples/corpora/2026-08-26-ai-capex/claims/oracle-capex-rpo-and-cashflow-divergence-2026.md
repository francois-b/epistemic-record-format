## Working notes

Oracle's fiscal 2026 results show capital expenditure and remaining performance obligations both growing several-fold year-over-year while free cash flow turned sharply negative, a divergence Oracle attributes to Cloud Infrastructure growth investment and partly offsets through debt and prepaid-contract financing.

Capex rose from $21.2B to $55.7B FY2025->FY2026 with $260B of additional off-balance-sheet lease commitments (acx-6); RPO rose from $138B to $638B, up 363% YoY (acx-5, acx-148); free cash flow was negative $23.7B for the fiscal year (acx-8), with net cash used for investing up $30.1B YoY, primarily the $34.4B capex increase partly offset by Ampere-stake sale proceeds (acx-64); Oracle raised $43B in debt and $5B in equity financing during the year to fund the program (acx-63). The other of the two per-company claims decomposed out of capex-hyperscaler-aggregate-2026-doubling, chosen alongside Microsoft because the two present nearly opposite cash-flow pictures at similar capex growth rates, which is exactly the texture that claim's aggregate figure cannot show. atoms_against carries Oracle's own countervailing claim that its $75B of prepaid and customer-supplied-hardware contract terms 'substantially reduces' the capital Oracle itself must raise (acx-7) -- in tension with the $43B of debt actually raised in the same fiscal year (acx-63), a tension this claim states rather than resolves.
