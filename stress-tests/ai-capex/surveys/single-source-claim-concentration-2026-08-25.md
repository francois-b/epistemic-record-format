Sought: which claims in the corpus would lose all of their supporting evidence if their one cited source turned out to be wrong, retracted, or unavailable -- the concentration risk the task brief asked this survey to check, run as search acts over the claim files' evidence lists rather than as a subjective read of "does this feel thin."

Act 1's headline number, 28 of 53 (53%), is larger than I expected walking in, and larger than it should be read at face value: more than half the corpus's claims are, mechanically, single-source. But act 2's classification is what makes that number usable rather than alarming. Once split, 18 of the 28 are single-source because the fact itself has one origin (a company's own filing, a grid operator's own auction result) -- concentration there is not a weakness, it is what citing a primary disclosure correctly looks like. The other 10 are single-source in the sense that actually matters for this survey's purpose: one person's or one team's reading of the world, with nothing else in this corpus checking it.

What surprised me: the MIT NANDA finding in notable_results. I expected single-source concentration to spread thinly across many different claims, one source each; instead, one source (the NANDA report) anchors three separate claims, including the claim (enterprise-roi-scarcity-supports-bubble-risk-reading) that is the corpus's most direct evidentiary bridge from ROI evidence into the central bubble-risk thesis. A reader who checked "does the ROI-scarcity argument have atoms_for from more than one place" claim by claim, without also checking whether those claims share an underlying source, could be misled into thinking the ROI case is broader than it is.

Where a possible claim could cite this: a claim about the corpus's own evidentiary structure -- for instance, that the enterprise-ROI-scarcity thread rests on a single study with no independent corroboration in this corpus -- could cite this survey rather than re-deriving the source-sharing fact by hand. Per this closing pass's brief, no such claim is minted here.

Coverage bounds: act 1 and act 3 are deterministic computations over the closed set of 53 claims and 151 atoms, so the 28/53 and 28-of-41 figures are exact and conclusive within the corpus as it stands; there is no sampling error to caveat. Act 2's primary-disclosure-versus-analyst-argument split is a reading, not a mechanical fact -- Zitron's tallies (used against several claims, not among the 28 here since those claims combine his atoms with others) sit closer to "analysis" than "disclosure" despite being built from disclosed figures, and a different author could draw that line elsewhere. The classification is stated plainly as this survey's judgment, consistent with how survey 2 flagged its own bull/bear sort the same way.
